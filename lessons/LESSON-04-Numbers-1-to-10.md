## Learning Each Number

### 1️⃣ 一 (yī) - One
- **Tone:** 1 (high and flat)
- **Shape:** One horizontal line (看起来像一 = looks like 1)
- **Pronunciation:** "yee"
- **Mnemonic:** It IS the number 1!

### 2️⃣ 二 (èr) - Two
- **Tone:** 4 (falling)
- **Shape:** Two horizontal lines stacked
- **Pronunciation:** "are" (like English "air" but shorter)
- **Character Story:** Literally looks like two lines = 2

### 3️⃣ 三 (sān) - Three
- **Tone:** 1 (high)
- **Shape:** Three horizontal lines
- **Pronunciation:** "sahn"
- **Character Story:** Three lines = 3

### 4️⃣ 四 (sì) - Four
- **Tone:** 4 (falling)
- **Shape:** Like a square with something inside
- **Pronunciation:** "suh" (rhymes with "buzz")
- **Tip:** Sounds like "death" in English → remember this and you won't forget 4!

### 5️⃣ 五 (wǔ) - Five
- **Tone:** 3 (dipping)
- **Shape:** 一 + 口 (one + box)
- **Pronunciation:** "woo"
- **Mnemonic:** "Woo hoo!" when you get 5!

### 6️⃣ 六 (liù) - Six
- **Tone:** 4 (falling)
- **Shape:** Looks like flowing water (early form of 水)
- **Pronunciation:** "leo-oh" or "lyew"
- **Tip:** The most different-looking number

### 7️⃣ 七 (qī) - Seven
- **Tone:** 1 (high)
- **Shape:** Kind of looks like a cross
- **Pronunciation:** "chee" (like "cheese" without the z)
- **Mnemonic:** "Chi" → 7

### 8️⃣ 八 (bā) - Eight
- **Tone:** 1 (high)
- **Shape:** Two lines spreading apart (like ∧ upside down)
- **Pronunciation:** "bah"
- **Mnemonic:** Looks like the number 8 opening up!

### 9️⃣ 九 (jiǔ) - Nine
- **Tone:** 3 (dipping)
- **Shape:** Looks like a hook
- **Pronunciation:** "jeo" (rhymes with "you")
- **Mnemonic:** Hook shape = fishing for 9

### 🔟 十 (shí) - Ten
- **Tone:** 2 (rising)
- **Shape:** A cross (one horizontal + one vertical)
- **Pronunciation:** "shuh"
- **Mnemonic:** The crossing lines = completeness = 10

## Tone Patterns for Numbers

**Critical Observation:** Numbers have a mixed tone pattern!

| Tones | Numbers | Pattern |
|-------|---------|---------|
| Tone 1 | 1, 3, 7, 8 | High and steady |
| Tone 2 | 10 | Rising up |
| Tone 3 | 5, 9 | Dipping down |
| Tone 4 | 2, 4, 6 | Falling down |

**Practice:** Say numbers in order: 一二三四五六七八九十

Notice how your voice goes: UP, DOWN, UP, DOWN, DIP, DOWN, UP, UP, DIP, UP

This creates a musical pattern!

**Practice:** Say this 10 times, focusing on tone accuracy

## Numbers in Real Sentences

### Age (你几岁？- How old are you?)

| Age | Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|-----|---------|--------|---------|
| 1 year old | 一岁 | yī suì | One year old |
| 5 years old | 五岁 | wǔ suì | Five years old |
| 10 years old | 十岁 | shí suì | Ten years old |

**Pattern:** Number + 岁 (suì) = age

**Full Sentence:**
- 我五岁 (wǒ wǔ suì) = I'm 5 years old
- 他十岁 (tā shí suì) = He's 10 years old

### Phone Numbers

Numbers are read one by one (not grouped like English):

**English:** "One-two-three-four-five-six-seven-eight-nine-zero"  
**Chinese:** 一、二、三、四、五、六、七、八、九、零 (zero = 零)

For example, the number 135:
- English: "One-thirty-five"
- Chinese: 一三五 (yī sān wǔ) - said individually

## Special Pronunciation Rules

### Rule 1: The Special "一" (One)

The character 一 (one) changes pronunciation depending on what comes after:

| Position | Tone Change | Example | New Tone |
|----------|-------------|---------|----------|
| Before Tone 4 | Becomes Tone 2 | 一个 (yí gè) | Tone 2 |
| Before Tone 1-3 | Becomes Tone 4 | 一天 (yì tiān) | Tone 4 |
| Final position | Stays Tone 1 | 第一 (dì yī) | Tone 1 |

**Why?** This makes speech flow naturally. You'll pick it up naturally with practice!

### Rule 2: Tone Sandhi Before 四 (Four)

When 十 comes before other numbers, it can shorten:
- 十一 (shíyī) = 11 (literally "ten one")
- 十二 (shì'èr) = 12
- 十三 (shísān) = 13

But when reading phone numbers or lists, read each character fully.

## Common Number Phrases for HSK1

### Questions & Answers

**How many? - 多少？(duōshao?)**
- 多少钱？(duōshao qián?) = How much money?
- 多少人？(duōshao rén?) = How many people?
- 你有多少个朋友？(nǐ yǒu duōshao ge péngyou?) = How many friends do you have?

**Answering with numbers:**
- 一个 (yī ge) = one (classifier + object)
- 两个 (liǎng ge) = two (alternate way to say 2 with counters)
- 十个 (shí ge) = ten

### Classifier System (Important!)

In Chinese, you can't say "5 friends" without a classifier. You must say "5 [measure word] friends"

**Number + 个 (ge) = the most common classifier**

Examples:
- 一个人 (yī ge rén) = one person
- 五个朋友 (wǔ ge péngyou) = five friends
- 十个学生 (shí ge xuésheng) = ten students

**More classifiers (for later):**
- 一只猫 (yī zhī māo) = one cat (个 vs 只 for animals)
- 一本书 (yī běn shū) = one book (个 vs 本 for books)

For now, just use 个 for everything!
