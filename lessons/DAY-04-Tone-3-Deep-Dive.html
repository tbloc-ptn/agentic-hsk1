Tone 3: Low Dipping

Master the third tone - the trickiest but most satisfying tone

✨ Today's Objectives

  • Understand Tone 3: dip LOW then rise slightly
  • Recognize Tone 3 by sound (usually the hardest to hear at first)
  • Understand why Tone 3 feels awkward
  • Practice 10+ Tone 3 words with proper dipping motion

Tone 3: Low Dipping (ˇ)

Key Characteristics:
  • Pitch: Dips LOW then rises slightly
  • Visual: ∨ (dipping down)
  • Symbol: ǎ ě ǐ ǒ ǔ
  • Feeling: Like expressing doubt or uncertainty ("Huh?")
  • Tone Mark: Caron / háček (ˇ) over the vowel
⚠️ Warning: Tone 3 is the HARDEST tone for beginners! It feels unnatural because you have to drop your pitch very low. This is completely normal. Most learners "get it" around Day 15-20.

Understanding Tone 3 Visually

Your voice pitch: starts at middle, DROPS LOW, then rises slightly at the end

↘️ DIP LOW ↗️

Go down, then slightly up

How to Produce Tone 3

  1. Start at middle pitch: Begin at a comfortable middle level
  2. Drop LOW: Go as low as you can comfortably (lower than your normal voice)
  3. Pause: Stay low for a moment
  4. Rise slightly: Bring it up just a little bit at the end
  5. Exaggerate at first: Make it VERY obvious until it feels natural

Why Tone 3 Feels Weird

Tone 3 is unnatural because:

  • In English, we rarely dip our pitch that low
  • It requires more effort than Tones 1, 2, and 4
  • The "dip low then rise" motion is awkward at first
  • It often gets confused with Tone 4 (falling) by beginners

But here's the good news: Once you practice it 10+ times, your mouth remembers the motion, and it becomes much easier!

Practice Exercises

🎯Exercise 1: Exaggerate Tone 3

Say these with HUGE dips (exaggerate!):

  • mǎ - Drop your voice super low, then rise slightly
  • nǐ - Same motion, make it obvious
  • wǒ - Go very low

Pro Tip: Exaggeration is your friend! Once it feels more natural, you can reduce the exaggeration.

🔄Exercise 2: Tone 1, 2, 3 Comparison

Say these in order and hear all three tones:

  1. mā (Tone 1 - FLAT, high) → má (Tone 2 - RISING) → mǎ (Tone 3 - DIPPING)
  2. tā (Tone 1) → tá (Tone 2) → tǎ (Tone 3)

Goal: Hear the clear difference between all three

📝Exercise 3: The "Question" Practice

Tone 3 often sounds like you're asking a confused question. Use this:

  1. In English, say "Huh?" with a confused tone (goes low then up)
  2. Now say mǎ? with the same motion
  3. They should feel similar!

🎤Exercise 4: Recording & Feedback

Record and compare:

  1. Record yourself saying: "mā má mǎ" (Tones 1, 2, 3 in sequence)
  2. Play it back
  3. Listen to native speaker saying the same
  4. Can you hear Tone 3 is different from 1 and 2?
