Lesson 1: Pinyin & The Four Tones

Welcome to your HSK1 journey! This lesson covers the absolute foundation of Mandarin Chinese: pinyin (拼音) and the four tones. Without mastering this, everything else will be difficult. The good news? You've got this—tones become natural with practice.

What is Pinyin?

Pinyin (拼音) literally means "spelled sound." It's the Romanization system used to write Chinese using the Latin alphabet. Here's why it matters:

Why Pinyin Matters for HSK1:
  • It shows you how to pronounce Chinese characters correctly
  • You'll use it to type Chinese on computers and phones
  • It's your bridge from English phonetics to Chinese sounds
  • HSK1 exams include pinyin recognition and writing

For example, the Chinese character 妈 (mother) is written in pinyin as mā. The small mark above the "a" is a tone mark—we'll explore these next.

The Four Tones of Mandarin Chinese

This is the critical part: Mandarin Chinese is a tonal language. The same syllable pronounced with different tones means completely different things. For instance, the sound "ma" has four distinct meanings:

1st Tone: High Flat (—)
妈 (mā)
Mother
📢 High, steady voice
2nd Tone: Rising (/)
麻 (má)
Hemp / Numb
📢 Rising, like asking a question
3rd Tone: Low Dip (∨)
马 (mǎ)
Horse
📢 Dip down, then slightly up
4th Tone: Falling (\)
骂 (mà)
To scold / Curse
📢 Falling, emphatic

🎯 Key Insight: Tones Are Not Optional

In English, we use tone of voice to convey emotion ("I love this!" vs. "I love this?"). In Mandarin, tone changes the actual word. Getting the tone wrong is like saying the wrong word entirely. Native speakers notice immediately.

Reading Tone Marks in Pinyin

Tone marks appear above the main vowel in a syllable. Here are the patterns:

Tone Mark Description Example
1st ā ē ī ō ū ü Flat line over vowel mā (妈 - mother)
2nd á é í ó ú ǘ Diagonal line rising right má (麻 - hemp)
3rd ǎ ě ǐ ǒ ǔ ǚ Checkmark (dip down) mǎ (马 - horse)
4th à è ì ò ù ǜ Diagonal line falling left mà (骂 - to scold)
💡 Pro Tip: Which Vowel Gets the Mark?

When a syllable has multiple vowels, follow this rule:

  1. If there's an "a" or "e", it gets the mark
  2. If there's "ou", the "o" gets the mark
  3. Otherwise, the mark goes on the last vowel

Example: In "nǐ" (你 - you), the mark is on "i" because there's no "a" or "e". In "hǎo" (好 - good), the mark is on "a".

Basic Pinyin Initials & Finals

Pinyin is built from two parts: an initial (the consonant at the start) and a final (the vowel sound). Here are some core ones you'll see in HSK1:

📝 What You've Learned

  • Pinyin is the Roman alphabet system for writing Chinese sounds
  • Mandarin has four tones, each marked differently in pinyin
  • Tone marks always go on the main vowel, following specific rules
  • Tones matter—wrong tone = wrong word
  • Each tone has a characteristic pitch shape (high, rising, dipping, falling)

Practice & Next Steps

Before moving to the next lesson, spend 5-10 minutes on these activities:

  1. Listen & Repeat: Go to mandarin.com/beginner/tones and listen to the four tones repeatedly. Repeat out loud—this trains your ear.
  2. Write It Out: On paper, write the pinyin for: 妈、麻、马、骂 (the "ma" family). Say each one aloud.
  3. Tone Counting Game: Every time you see pinyin this week, quickly identify the tone number before reading it.
