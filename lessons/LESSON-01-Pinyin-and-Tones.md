# Lesson 1: Pinyin & The Four Tones

## Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will:
- Understand what pinyin is and why it's essential
- Know all 4 tones in Mandarin Chinese
- Recognize tone marks in written pinyin
- Practice basic tone pronunciation

---

## What is Pinyin?

**Pinyin** (拼音) = "spell sound" - it's the system of romanizing (writing in Latin letters) Mandarin Chinese sounds.

### Why Learn Pinyin?
1. **Pronunciation Guide:** Shows you how to say Chinese characters
2. **Foundation:** Master tones early → easier character learning later
3. **International Standard:** Used worldwide in Chinese learning

### Key Concept
Each Chinese character has ONE sound = ONE syllable (usually)

Examples:
- 妈 (mā) = one syllable
- 中国 (Zhōngguó) = two syllables (zhōng + guó)

---

## The Four Tones of Mandarin

Mandarin has **4 tones** + 1 neutral tone. Tone = pitch/melody of how you say a syllable.

### Tone 1: High Flat (¯)
- **Pitch:** High and stays high
- **Symbol:** ā, ē, ī, ō, ū
- **Visual:** ─── (straight line)
- **Example:** 妈 (mā) = mother
- **Feeling:** Like a siren that doesn't change pitch

### Tone 2: Rising (´)
- **Pitch:** Starts middle, goes UP
- **Symbol:** á, é, í, ó, ú
- **Visual:** ╱ (going up)
- **Example:** 麻 (má) = hemp
- **Feeling:** Like asking a question in English "What?"

### Tone 3: Low Dipping (ˇ)
- **Pitch:** Dips LOW then rises slightly
- **Symbol:** ǎ, ě, ǐ, ǒ, ǔ
- **Visual:** ∨ (dipping down)
- **Example:** 马 (mǎ) = horse
- **Feeling:** Like you're expressing doubt "Huh?"

### Tone 4: Falling (`)
- **Pitch:** High, falls DOWN quickly
- **Symbol:** à, è, ì, ò, ù
- **Visual:** ╲ (falling down)
- **Example:** 骂 (mà) = to scold
- **Feeling:** Like giving a command "Stop!"

### Neutral Tone (no mark)
- **Pitch:** Light, fast, no stress
- **Symbol:** a, e, i, o, u (no mark)
- **Example:** 妈妈 (māma) = mother (second 妈 is neutral)

---

## The Famous "MA" Example

The syllable "MA" with 4 different tones = 4 different meanings:

| Tone | Pinyin | Character | Meaning |
|------|--------|-----------|---------|
| Tone 1 | mā | 妈 | Mother |
| Tone 2 | má | 麻 | Hemp/Numb |
| Tone 3 | mǎ | 马 | Horse |
| Tone 4 | mà | 骂 | To scold |

**This is why tones matter!** 🎯

## Pinyin Rules

### 1. Vowels (a, e, i, o, u, ü)
- **a, o, e** are always loud and clear
- **i** sounds like "ee" (like in "see")
- **u** sounds like "oo" (like in "boot")
- **ü** sounds like "ü" in French (round your lips, say "ee")

### 2. Consonants
- Most consonants sound similar to English, but:
  - **x** = "sh" (like in "sheet")
  - **q** = "ch" (like in "cheese")
  - **z** = "ds" (like in "suds")
  - **c** = "ts" (like in "cats")
  - **zh** = "j" (like in "judge")
  - **ch** = "ch" (like in "church")
  - **sh** = "sh" (like in "shore")
  - **r** = like English "r" but softer

### 3. Special Combinations
- **ao** = "ow" (like in "how")
- **iu** = "yo" (like in "you")
- **ian** = "yen" (like in "yes")
- **ang** = "ung" (like in "sung")

## Tone Marks: Where Do They Go?

The tone mark always goes on the **main vowel**:

**Rule:** The tone mark follows this order of priority:
1. If **a** or **e** exists, mark it
2. If **o** exists, mark it
3. If **i** and **u** together, mark the **u**
4. Otherwise, mark the last vowel

Examples:
- m**ā** (a gets the mark)
- h**ǎo** (a gets the mark, even though o is there)
- l**ǔ** (u gets the mark over i)
- m**ē**i (e gets the mark, not i)

## Practice Exercises

### Exercise 1: Identify the Tone
Listen to each syllable and write which tone it is (1, 2, 3, or 4):

**Audio Simulation** (in real practice, you'd listen):
- mā → Tone 1
- má → Tone 2
- mǎ → Tone 3
- mà → Tone 4

Try these:
- tā → ? 
- tá → ?
- tǎ → ?
- tà → ?

**Answer:** 1, 2, 3, 4

### Exercise 2: Write the Pinyin
Add tone marks to these syllables:

1. ba (tone 1) → bā
2. ba (tone 2) → bá
3. ba (tone 3) → bǎ
4. ba (tone 4) → bà

Try these:
- li (tone 1) → ?
- li (tone 2) → ?
- li (tone 3) → ?
- li (tone 4) → ?

**Answer:** lī, lí, lǐ, lì

### Exercise 3: Say It Out Loud!

Repeat 5 times each, exaggerating the tone:
1. **Tone 1 (High):** mā mā mā mā mā (like a constant high note)
2. **Tone 2 (Rising):** má má má má má (like asking "what?")
3. **Tone 3 (Dipping):** mǎ mǎ mǎ mǎ mǎ (go low then rise)
4. **Tone 4 (Falling):** mà mà mà mà mà (like a command)

## Key Takeaways

✅ Pinyin is the romanization system for Mandarin  
✅ The 4 tones are: High (1), Rising (2), Dipping (3), Falling (4)  
✅ Tone = meaning (mā ≠ má ≠ mǎ ≠ mà)  
✅ Tone marks go on the main vowel  
✅ Consistent practice = tone recognition by Week 2
