Tone 2: Rising (´)

Key Characteristics:
  • Pitch: Starts MIDDLE, goes UP
  • Visual: ╱ (going up)
  • Symbol: á é í ó ú
  • Feeling: Like asking "What?" in English with rising intonation
  • Tone Mark: Accent mark (´) over the vowel

Understanding Tone 2 Visually

Imagine your voice pitch as a graph. Tone 2 looks like this:

Starts low-middle, rises to high!

How to Produce Tone 2

  1. Start at middle pitch: Say a comfortable middle note (not too high, not too low)
  2. Slide UP: As you say the vowel, gradually go up in pitch
  3. End HIGH: Finish at a naturally high pitch
  4. Sound like you're asking: "What?" "Really?" should feel similar
  5. Practice repeatedly: Say it 10+ times until it feels natural

Tone 1 vs. Tone 2 Comparison

This is CRITICAL because many beginners confuse these two:

Feature Tone 1 (High) Tone 2 (Rising)
Pitch Pattern High and FLAT (doesn't change) Starts middle, RISES to high
Visual ─── (horizontal line) ╱ (diagonal line going up)
Tone Mark ¯ (macron / bar) ´ (accent / acute)
Example mā (mother) - stable má (hemp) - questioning
English Feeling Making a statement "Hello." Asking a question "Hello?"
⚠️ Common Mistake: Beginners often make Tone 1 too high or make Tone 2 too flat. Remember: Tone 1 is FLAT (no movement), Tone 2 MOVES (rises)!

🎤Exercise 2: Tone 2 Pronunciation

  • má - Rise up like you're asking "What?"
  • lái - Same rising motion
  • èr - Middle to high
  • kāi - Wait, this might be Tone 1! (it is)
